# Design tables can shape the other seven gauges

Yes, but only some of the seven reservoirs can honestly get a shaped, share-of-storage gauge like Mazar's, and remote sensing will not get them there. The workable path is a **two-point power-law curve built from CELEC's published design figures** (total volume, useful volume, the operating levels and the riverbed elevation). Run a satellite surface-area check on the larger pools, and read the few theses and papers that hold real surveyed curves. Global reservoir-geometry products barely cover these dams. Pre-impoundment DEMs cannot resolve operating bands that are 3–14 m thick. Water-balance fitting works only where storage changes are large relative to flow errors, which is true at Mazar and false at the small ponds. One fact makes the job easier than it looks: **a gauge that shows the share of stored water depends only on the curve's shape, not its scale.** A curve that is wrong in absolute hm³ by a third can still place the "percent full" mark within a few points. Amaluza and Minas San Francisco can be shaped with modest effort. Manduriacu and Agoyán can get design-flagged two-point curves, though a shape barely changes their gauge reading. Delsitanisagua, Sopladora and the Coca Codo Sinclair compensation pond should not be drawn as storage gauges at all. Mazar's own fit (≈206 hm³ live) sits ~100 hm³ below the design useful volume of 309 hm³. The evidence localises that gap to the top ~30 m, and a single full-pool Sentinel-2 scene can settle it. Caveat: this research could not open most full texts, so many figures below come from search excerpts and are marked as needing a full-text read.

## A storage gauge needs shape, and shape matters only for deep bands

The gauge's percentage is V(h)/V(max) within the operating band. Any error that multiplies the whole curve by a constant cancels out, so the share depends only on how area grows with level. For a power-law reservoir, A = a(h − h₀)^b, so it depends only on the exponent b and the zero-area elevation h₀. How far a shaped gauge departs from a straight level bar depends on how thick the operating band is compared with the full depth of the pool. The table below shows this. It is this report's arithmetic, using the published bands and riverbed elevations derived from dam crest minus dam height. The site already uses CELEC's declared bands: Mazar 2100–2153, Amaluza 1970–1991 and Minas San Francisco 783.33–792.86 m (`/home/user/hydro-look/data/reference/thresholds.csv`).

| Reservoir (band used) | Assumed shape b | Storage share at 25 / 50 / 75 % of level |
|---|---|---|
| Mazar 2100–2153, project fit (b = 2.5, h₀ 2036.66) | 2.5 | **12.8 / 32.4 / 60.8 %** |
| Mazar 2100–2153, design two-point (h₀ ≈ 2000) | 2.15 | 17.0 / 39.0 / 66.4 % |
| Amaluza 1970–1991 (h₀ ≈ 1824) | 1.5 – 3.4 | 21–23 / 44–48 / 71–73 % |
| Minas San Francisco 783.33–792.86 (h₀ ≈ 741) | 1.0 – 2.5 | 20–23 / 44–48 / 70–73 % |
| Manduriacu 489.4–492.5 (h₀ ≈ 454) | 1.0 – 3.0 | 23–24 / 47–49 / 73–74 % |
| Agoyán 1637–1651 observed (h₀ ≈ 1610) | 1.0 – 3.0 | 15–21 / 35–45 / 63–71 % |
| CCS compensation 1216–1229.5 (h₀ ≈ 1180) | 1.0 – 3.0 | 17–22 / 38–46 / 66–72 % |
| Delsitanisagua 1469–1491 observed (h₀ ≈ 1460) | 1.0 – 3.0 | **4–15 / 17–36 / 45–65 %** |

Two lessons follow. At Mazar the shape really matters: a level bar at mid-band reads 50 %, but the reservoir holds only **32–39 %** of its live water. The fit and the design-derived curve disagree by only 4–7 points, even though they disagree by a third on absolute volume. At Amaluza, Minas San Francisco and Manduriacu, the operating band is a thin slice near the top of a deep valley. There the shaped gauge moves the reading by only 2–7 points from the level bar, and the uncertainty in b moves it by about 3 more. A shaped gauge there is honest and cheap, but it is mostly a visual and interpretive upgrade, not a large correction. At Delsitanisagua the "band" is really the observed range of flushing drawdowns across most of the dam height. The share swings by 20 points depending on an unknown exponent, so drawing it shaped would display a guess.

The same arithmetic explains why the water-balance fit failed on the small ponds. The fit infers area from how much level moves per unit of net flow. The Coca Codo Sinclair conveyance diverts about **222 m³/s** ([UTN](https://energiasrenovables.utn.edu.ec/museo/coca-codo-sinclair.html); [Sinohydro](https://sinohydroecuador.com/asi-funciona-la-central-coca-codo-sinclair/)), about 19 hm³ a day, into a pond of about **0.8 hm³**. That pond is roughly one hour of design flow. A 1 % error in the flow terms is 0.19 hm³ a day, which is several metres of level in a ~0.06 hm³-per-metre pond. That matches the 4–5 m level errors and the absurd 82 hm³ storage the fit returned. At Mazar, with ~100 m³/s and 1.5–7 km² of surface, the same 1 % error moves the level by centimetres. Water-balance fitting is a large-reservoir tool. Its failure at the seven is a statement about measurement noise, not proof that no curve can exist.

## Mazar's 103 hm³ gap sits in the top 30 metres

The design numbers are internally consistent. The total at the 2153 m normal maximum is **410 hm³**. Of that, **101 hm³ lies below the 2098 m minimum**, which leaves **309 hm³ useful** between 2098 and 2153 m ([project description reproduced on buenastareas](https://www.buenastareas.com/ensayos/Proyecto-Hidroel%C3%A9ctrico-Mazar/1313001.html); [CELEC news, 410 hm³ total](https://www.celec.gob.ec/noticias/presa-mazar-alcanza-su-cota-maxima-y-el-gobierno-del-nuevo-ecuador-reafirma-un-servicio-energetico-eficiente/); [Primicias, 309 usable / 101 below minimum](https://www.primicias.ec/noticias/economia/hidroelectrica-mazar-embalse-reservas-minimas-cortes-luz/)). The 309 figure should be treated as a secondary-source design value. Its original CELEC/Hidropaute document is not online, and the cleanest copy is on a student-essay site. The project's fit is A = 48.3 (h − 2036.66)^2.5 with the exponent fixed at 2.5, a 0.69 m level RMSE over 2,016 days (`/home/user/hydro-look/public/api/forecast.json`). It gives **≈206 hm³ for 2100–2153 m and 234 hm³ down to its own zero-area datum**. That is a third below design live storage and well short of the 410 hm³ total.

The interesting part is where the gap lives. Torres Campozano & Bermúdez Zambrano (2024) estimated Mazar's storage from Sentinel-2 and DEMs. They put **2111.45 m at 30.97 %** and ~2123 m at **37.47 % of total capacity** ([UG journal](https://revistas.ug.edu.ec/index.php/cna/en/article/view/41)). If their total is 410 hm³ with 101 hm³ dead, those points imply about 26 hm³ between 2098 and 2111 m and 53 hm³ between 2098 and 2123 m. The project's fit gives **25 hm³ and 58 hm³** for the same intervals, which is close agreement in the lower band from an independent method. The disagreement is in the 2123–2153 band. There the paper's numbers imply ~256 hm³, while the fit holds ~152 hm³. A design-consistent curve also predicts a larger full-pool surface. Solving a two-point power law through 410 hm³ at 2153 m and 101 hm³ at 2098 m, with a riverbed of 2000–2010 m, gives b ≈ 1.9–2.1 and **A(2153) ≈ 8.3–8.4 km²**. The fit predicts **7.05 km²**. That difference of about 20 % is far above Sentinel-2 shoreline error for a reservoir this size (all of this paragraph's curve figures are this report's arithmetic).

Sedimentation cannot close the gap alone. CELEC gives the Paute's sediment inflow to Amaluza, just downstream, as **2.25 hm³/yr** ([CELEC Sur, Paute Molino](https://www.celec.gob.ec/celecsur/informacion-tecnica/central-hidroelectrica-paute-molino/)). Mazar now traps that load, and over ~16 years since impoundment it amounts to at most ~36 hm³, about a third of the shortfall. That is true even if every grain landed in the live zone, which delta deposition at the reservoir tail can partly do. The rest must come from the flow terms or the design curve itself. A uniform bias in net flow scales the fitted area one-for-one, so a ~30 % underestimate of net inflow, for example from ungauged lateral inflow or a turbine-flow conversion factor, would reproduce the gap exactly. This points to a practical rule. **Trust the fit's shape for the gauge percentage, but do not publish its absolute hm³ as fact.** Treat 309 hm³ as the validation target for the absolute scale, and decide between them with one test. Measure the water surface in a clear Sentinel-2 scene on a day the gauge reads 2153 m (CELEC has announced Mazar reaching its maximum level, so such days exist in the record). About 7 km² supports the fit; about 8.3 km² or more supports design. Scenes near 2111 m and 2123 m should read about 2.3 km² and 3.3 km² under the fit. The UCACUE 2021 thesis on Mazar–Amaluza volume optimisation ([UCACUE](https://dspace.ucacue.edu.ec/bitstreams/15b8db52-9203-477b-8928-fecc5b6264f4/download)) and the full Torres & Bermúdez paper ([PDF](https://revistas.ug.edu.ec/index.php/cna/en/article/download/41/60/121)) need a full-text read, because they may already contain areas or a cota-volumen relation.

The two-point solutions are this report's arithmetic and need confirmation. They come out physically plausible, which is itself a useful check. Minas San Francisco gives b ≈ 1.6 and a full-pool area of about 0.73 km². Agoyán gives b ≈ 2.4 and about 0.15 km². Amaluza's design figures give b ≈ 3.4 and about 3.2 km² at 1991 m. Manduriacu is the one place where an independent check is possible. Its published 70.5 ha, 10.3 hm³ and 38.5 m depth to the riverbed imply b ≈ 1.6, and a live volume of **≈2.0 hm³ in the 489.4–492.5 m normal band**.

Amaluza's numbers come with a large caveat. Capacity loss was **36.4 hm³ by the late 1990s** (Jerves 1999, via [Mora et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262674494_Actualizacion_del_conocimiento_sobre_la_produccion_de_sedimentos_a_diversas_escalas_en_la_cuenca_hidrografica_del_Rio_Paute)), and a press report put the loss at ~50 hm³ or 42 % before Mazar ([El Comercio, press](https://www.elcomercio.com/actualidad/sedimentos-llenan-42-del-embalse/)). CELEC still publishes the nominal 120/100 hm³, even though it runs bathymetric surveys twice a year. The real Amaluza curve almost certainly exists in that record. The publicly reachable versions are the hypsometric curve used to calibrate Sánchez-Juny et al. (2023), built from 30 cross-sections surveyed 1983–2013 ([Ingeniería del Agua](https://polipapers.upv.es/index.php/IA/article/view/20150)), Larriva's 2023 UPC PhD thesis ([TDX](https://www.tdx.cat/handle/10803/690318)), and Mora's 2005 annex of accumulated volumes ([UCuenca](https://rest-dspace.ucuenca.edu.ec/server/api/core/bitstreams/5bf9f31b-dc3c-4d48-87b0-5efdd59e0d19/content)). Minas San Francisco is even better placed. Ochoa-García (2021) **computed cota-área and cota-volumen curves for this exact reservoir from a 2017 LiDAR survey and a 2019 sonar bathymetry** ([Revista de Teledetección PDF](https://polipapers.upv.es/index.php/raet/article/viewFile/15303/14167)). That is a measured as-built curve on a public journal page. None of these could be opened in this session, and they are the highest-value reads.

Global geometry datasets add little. Only three open products give per-reservoir area–depth–volume curves. ReGeom fits one of five geometric shapes to a reservoir's catalogue capacity and area ([Zenodo 1322884](https://zenodo.org/records/1322884)). GRDL reconstructs bathymetry with deep learning, at a **median nRMSE of 20.6 % (area–storage) and 22.1 % (area–level)** ([Hao et al. 2024, WRR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023WR035781)). 3D-LAKES derives curves from ICESat-2 and Landsat, with interpolated L2 layers that "may contain errors" ([Zenodo 13107867](https://zenodo.org/records/13107867)). ReGeom and GRDL are keyed to GRanD, which targets reservoirs above 0.1 km³ (100 hm³) ([McGill Hydrolab](https://wp.geog.mcgill.ca/hydrolab/?p=139)). Only Mazar and Amaluza plausibly qualify, and their membership was not verified because no attribute table could be downloaded. ReGeom is anchored on the same design capacity the project already has, so for Amaluza it can offer at most an independent shape guess at ~20 % error, not a sediment-corrected volume. The cheapest membership check is the login-free Global Dam Watch CSV on figshare ([GDW v1.0](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Global_Dam_Watch_database_version_1_0/25988293)).

## Satellites and pre-dam DEMs resolve only the larger pools

Pairing satellite surface area with the gauged level is the most powerful independent method, because the level side is error-free. Published storage accuracies are **3–15 % NRMSE** for large reservoirs ([Gao et al. 2012](https://edcftp.cr.usgs.gov/project/Intl/Iraq/Reservoir%20Monitoring%20Literature/WRR1.pdf)) and **10–16 %** for hybrid Landsat–DEM methods ([Avisse et al. 2017](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/21/6445/2017/)). The method works only when area changes across the band by much more than the shoreline error. In mountains it also suffers terrain-shadow false water ([Water 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/W13070945)), and small water bodies are systematically underestimated ([Remote Sensing 2025](https://doaj.org/article/3e8ba48190a8455f910b385e4dacef89)). Mazar passes easily: its area roughly quadruples from 2100 to 2153 m. Amaluza, at roughly 3 km² with a 21 m band, should pass. Minas San Francisco (~0.7 km², 9.5 m band) is marginal: its area changes by about 30 % across the band, against a Sentinel-2 error of perhaps 5–10 %. Manduriacu's 3.1 m normal band changes area by only about 13 %. At Delsitanisagua, CCS (a few hectares) and the Sopladora chamber, the change is lost in mixed pixels. Even so, one full-pool Sentinel-2 area is a cheap sanity check on each two-point curve, especially Manduriacu's published 70.5 ha.

Pre-impoundment DEM contour integration has a timing advantage. SRTM/NASADEM (February 2000) predates every reservoir except Amaluza (1983) and Agoyán (1987), and Copernicus GLO-30 (2010–2015 acquisition) predates the post-2015 dams ([Copernicus DEM handbook](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2024-06/geo1988-copernicusdem-spe-002_producthandbook_i5.0.pdf)). Vertical error is the obstacle. Global DEMs show **6.6–6.7 m RMSE in steep forested terrain** ([IJDE 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17538947.2022.2094002)). FABDEM's canopy removal cuts forest error roughly in half ([EGU22-8994](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-8994.html)). SRTM-derived volume curves for small dams err by **under 20 % on smooth terrain but over 150 % on rugged terrain** ([Sci. Rep. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-30483-7)). When the DEM error is larger than the operating band itself (Manduriacu's 3.1 m, Agoyán's 6 m), the DEM can describe the valley's overall shape, and so check the exponent b, but it cannot measure the live slice. The datum also has to be solved empirically, by matching DEM contours to satellite shorelines at gauged levels. Each metre of misalignment shifts Mazar's live volume by about 2.7 % (this report's arithmetic). The DEM route is therefore worth running only on Mazar, as an arbiter for the lower canyon and the 2036 m vs ~2000 m zero-area question, and as a b-check on Minas San Francisco and Manduriacu.

SWOT swath altimetry and the level-only archives (DAHITI, Hydroweb) add nothing here. The project already has daily gauge levels, and SWOT area is noisy over narrow water bodies ([Das et al. 2025](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024EA004178)).

## Conclusion

The question changes once you notice the gauge reports a ratio. The project does not need accurate hm³ to draw honest shaped gauges. It needs the right exponent over the right band, and for most of these reservoirs the band is thin enough that almost any plausible exponent gives the same reading within a few points. So the real deliverable for Amaluza, Minas San Francisco and Manduriacu is less "a curve" than "a defensible statement that storage share ≈ level share ± a few points, and here is why". The ponds where shape would matter most, Delsitanisagua and the Coca Codo compensator, are exactly the ones too small to measure, and for them the right graphic says "this is about an hour of water", not "47 % full".

The Mazar tension turns out to be an opportunity. The fit and the design figures agree in the lower band, disagree in the upper 30 m, and predict full-pool areas 20 % apart. A single cloud-free Sentinel-2 scene can therefore validate or overturn the project's only fitted curve. If the scene supports design, the fix is a scale change that leaves every published percentage nearly intact. If it supports the fit, the project has evidence that Mazar's live storage is a third smaller than the figure the press repeats, and that finding would deserve its own page.
